RESPONSE TO THE AUTHOR-REQUESTED MOCK REVIEW
Current manuscript: Detector-Assisted Annotation Audits: Capture Gaps, Count Estimation,
and Review Allocation. Revision: review-response-v1, 11 November 2026 (New York).

This is a response to a private AI-assisted pre-submission assessment, not a response
to an IEEE editorial decision. All changes use existing evidence. No new detector
inference, performance simulation, human labeling, dataset or paid resource was used.

MAJOR COMMENT 1: COMPARATOR ADEQUACY
Resolved for the demonstrated gap. Main Section VI-D and Table 5; supplement S12.2.

The exact same public-input allocation diagnostic is now included in all 24 retained
conditions. It censuses the >=2-proposal stratum when feasible while reserving two
reviews in each other nonempty stratum, then allocates the remainder proportionally.
Its rule consumes no outcome information or uncharged fitting labels. Its selection
was post hoc, which is disclosed. The paper does not claim a new method or prospective
selection validity. All risks were verified using exact rational arithmetic.

The practical recommendation changed. Original I/Faster PPAT benefits survive the
new comparator, with MSE ratios 0.581 and 0.798 on COCO and VOC. However, uncapped
COCO has PPAT/comparator ratio 1.179 [1.016,1.345]; the simple allocation has 15.2%
lower point risk. A 93.4% improvement over the original fitted-power baseline does
not establish PPAT as the preferred practical choice. Across all 24 conditions,
14 adjusted comparisons favor PPAT, seven favor allocation, and three are inconclusive.
The allocation has lower point risk in eight conditions, including one inconclusive.

The [0,1] fitted-calibration restriction and uncapped high-stratum oracle coefficient
1.846 are disclosed. The oracle is explanatory, not a deployable baseline. No claim
is made against unrestricted fitted calibration or every adaptive Neyman design.

MAJOR COMMENT 2: ALIGNED INFORMATION AND ESTIMATION EVIDENCE
Resolved for the scoped empirical claim. Main Section V-B and Figure 2; supplement S12.1.

The capture plot now uses the actual N/U/H omission sets used by the count-estimation
study, with equal within-frame denominators. All 800,000 saved public-feature rows,
8,000 full-reference detector matchings and 800 deletion blocks were reconstructed.
Capture is explicitly an uncapped omitted-node diagnostic, not retrieval precision/
recall; the bridge to capped estimation is the actual proxy and residual decomposition.

Primary natural/uniform capture gaps are 17.473 and 16.610 percentage points. Exact
fixed-difference risk ratios are 1.01893 and 0.96471; variance/covariance changes
nearly cancel. The same control sets yield descriptive PPAT ratios 1.22151 and
1.25029. Inclusive RetinaNet shows larger gaps and different risk responses.
The changed claims are endpoint-, estimator- and detector-specific. The manuscript
does not claim no synthetic optimism, a novel variance theorem, or equivalence from
a nonsignificant result. The original material-harm gate remains FAILED.

MINOR COMMENT 3: PRACTICAL DECISION AND ABSOLUTE ERROR
Resolved by reporting and scope correction. Main V-A, VI-A and VII.
Root MSE 34.97/600 and 22.53/177 corresponds to 5.8% and 12.7% of the operational
totals. Bias and 90th-percentile absolute error remain reported. Planning the scale
of annotation-review effort is a possible use, not a demonstrated downstream benefit.
No application-specific tolerance, human-time saving or natural-reference confidence
certificate was measured. Accurate selected-image measurement remains a requirement;
the callback implementation does not establish single-reviewer accuracy.

MINOR COMMENT 4: BOOTSTRAP TAIL PRECISION
Resolved numerically, without claiming better statistical coverage. Main IV-C,
updated Tables 2/4/5 and supplement S6, S10 and S12.3.
Two independent million-resample streams per cell are pooled to two million.
All 84 contrasts satisfy the frozen 2% endpoint-deviation and classification check;
maximum relative endpoint deviation is 0.16481%. The 48-family lower tail is now
near order statistic 1,042 rather than approximately five. Separate 12-, 48- and
post hoc 24-contrast families are explicit. Fifty blocks remain the statistical
basis; selection, reference and population uncertainty are not removed.
The former 39/7/2 secondary classification changes to 40/6/2 at a borderline
IGNORE/VOC/strict/Faster comparison. Both the old record and this change are disclosed.
