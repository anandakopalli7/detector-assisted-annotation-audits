WHAT THE PAPER NOW CONTRIBUTES
1. A controlled empirical finding on operational annotation counts. The natural
   omitted-node capture disadvantage is measured on the exact same matched controls
   as count estimation: gaps of 17.5/16.6 percentage points coexist with fixed-difference
   risk ratios 1.019/0.965, while PPAT ratios are 1.222/1.250. This is not merely
   comparing different metrics on unrelated groups. It identifies when the change
   in omitted identity does and does not translate to aggregate count risk.
2. A practically consequential comparison correction. The uncapped PPAT advantage
   of 93.4% over one proportional-allocation baseline reverses against a public-input
   allocation using exactly the same review budget: PPAT's risk is 17.9% higher.
   The paper therefore teaches a concrete audit-design lesson about allocation,
   rather than selling an already-published estimator as universally superior.
3. Reproducible operational boundaries. Original primary benefits, policy changes,
   small-budget failures, reference limitations and the failed directional hypothesis
   are retained. A selected-image callback makes the fixed procedure executable
   without supplying the whole outcome vector; reference quality remains external.

WHY THE DECISION CHANGES
The earlier decision rested on two demonstrated shortcomings, not on a journal
requirement to invent an algorithm. First, a feasible missing comparator contradicted
the broad practical recommendation. It is now evaluated and the recommendation
changes when it loses. Second, the information and estimation contrasts were not
aligned. They now use the same omission sets, actual proxies and exact risk
decomposition, and disclose where other estimators suffer synthetic optimism.
The resulting scientific lesson is supported by evidence rather than favorable
framing of a routine PPAT application. An empirical result can warrant review even
when its general statistical mechanism is classical.

RESIDUAL LIMITATIONS AND WHY THEY DO NOT TRIGGER NEW EXPERIMENTS HERE
Only person annotations, two related detectors, two confirmation frames and one
source-reference pipeline are studied. This limits scope but does not invalidate
the stated finite-frame contrast. Additional datasets would be needed for broad
generalization, which is not claimed.
The allocation rule was selected post hoc. Its risk on these fixed frames is valid;
it is not a validated prospective method-selection policy. No unrestricted-calibration
or full adaptive Neyman superiority claim survives in the text.
Capture is a fixed-matching node diagnostic, not a recreation of a complete retrieval
PR benchmark. Matching ties, reference incompleteness, count caps and policy choices
remain explicit. The exact proxy/risk analysis bridges that diagnostic to the chosen
estimand without equating uncapped instance capture with capped image counts.
No human-time study, downstream benefit or natural-reference interval coverage is
available. The paper states those limits instead of treating code correctness as
human measurement validation.
None of those omissions contradicts a central written claim. I therefore do not
recommend another experiment within this bounded revision. A future reviewer could
reasonably request broader validation; that possibility has not been eliminated.
